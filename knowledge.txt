=== NATIONAL HEALTH POLICY 2017 KEY EXCERPTS ===

India's National Health Policy 2017 aims to achieve Universal Health Coverage (UHC) and increase government health expenditure to 2.5% of GDP by 2025. Key targets include:
- Reduce Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) to below 100 by 2020, and below 70 by 2025
- Reduce Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) to below 28 by 2019
- Reduce Under-5 Mortality Rate to 23 by 2025
- Achieve full immunization coverage of 90% by 2025
- Increase health expenditure by government as percentage of GDP from 1.15% to 2.5% by 2025
- Increase state sector health spending to more than 8% of their budget by 2020
- Establish Health and Wellness Centers to provide comprehensive primary care

=== AYUSHMAN BHARAT SCHEME ===

Ayushman Bharat has two components:
1. Health and Wellness Centers (HWCs): Transform 150,000 Sub-Health Centers and Primary Health Centers into HWCs providing comprehensive primary healthcare including maternal and child health, communicable diseases, mental health, oral health, elderly care, and NCD screening.
2. Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PMJAY): Provides health insurance coverage of Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care hospitalization. Covers approximately 12 crore families (50 crore beneficiaries). Over 60% empaneled hospitals are private. Cashless and paperless treatment at point of care.

=== WHO HEALTHCARE BENCHMARKS ===

World Health Organization recommended minimums:
- Doctor-to-population ratio: 1 per 1,000 population (India average: 0.7)
- Hospital beds: 3 per 1,000 population (India average: 0.5 public + 1.4 private)
- Nurses and midwives: 3 per 1,000 population (India average: 1.7)
- Health spending: Minimum 5% of GDP on health
- ICU beds: 5-12 per 100,000 population (India average: 2.5)
- Immunization coverage: 95% for herd immunity for most diseases
- Emergency response time: Under 15 minutes in urban, 30 minutes in rural

=== NATIONAL IMMUNIZATION SCHEDULE (INDIA) ===

Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) covers:
- Birth: BCG, OPV-0, Hepatitis B birth dose
- 6 weeks: OPV-1, Pentavalent-1, Rotavirus-1, fIPV-1, PCV-1
- 10 weeks: OPV-2, Pentavalent-2, Rotavirus-2
- 14 weeks: OPV-3, Pentavalent-3, Rotavirus-3, fIPV-2, PCV-2
- 9 months: Measles-Rubella-1, JE-1 (endemic areas), PCV booster
- 16-24 months: Measles-Rubella-2, JE-2, DPT booster-1, OPV booster
- 5-6 years: DPT booster-2
- 10 years: Td (Tetanus-Diphtheria)
- 16 years: Td booster

India requires approximately 50+ crore vaccine doses annually for its immunization program.
Cold chain infrastructure includes: 28,000+ ILRs (Ice Lined Refrigerators), 24,000+ Deep Freezers, 84,000+ Cold Boxes.

=== SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT BEST PRACTICES ===

Healthcare Supply Chain in India follows a 4-tier system:
Tier 1: National/Central warehouses (GMSD - Government Medical Store Depot) - 7 depots
Tier 2: State Drug Warehouses - 36 (one per state/UT)
Tier 3: District Drug Stores - 750+
Tier 4: Facility-level stores (PHC/CHC/DH)

Key principles:
1. Essential Drug List: India maintains NLEM (National List of Essential Medicines) with 384 drugs
2. Procurement: Central procurement through HLL Lifecare, CMSS (Central Medical Services Society)
3. Distribution: State-managed through drug warehouses
4. Cold Chain: Specialized for vaccines, insulin, and blood products
5. Quality: Testing at CDSCO-approved labs before distribution
6. Emergency: Strategic Health Operation Center (SHOC) for rapid deployment

=== BUDGET ALLOCATION FRAMEWORKS ===

Government of India health budget allocation follows:
1. National Health Mission (NHM): Largest component (~30%)
   - NRHM (rural) + NUHM (urban)
   - Flexible pool for RCH, communicable diseases, NCD
   - State-specific RoP (Record of Proceedings) based allocation
2. PMJAY: ~8% of health budget
3. AIIMS/INIs: ~5%
4. Medical Education: ~5%
5. Autonomous Bodies (ICMR, NCDC, FSSAI): ~4%
6. Capital Projects (new hospitals/colleges): ~10%
7. CSS (Centrally Sponsored Schemes): ~30%
8. Administrative: ~8%

State allocation formula under NHM:
- Population weight: 40%
- Disease burden: 20%
- Infrastructure gap: 20%
- Performance incentive: 10%
- Innovation/special needs: 10%

=== HEALTHCARE 3-TIER ARCHITECTURE ===

India's public healthcare follows a 3-tier architecture:
PRIMARY LEVEL:
- Sub-Centers: 1 per 5,000 (rural), 1 per 10,000 (urban). Staffed by ANM. Currently: 160,000+
- Primary Health Centers (PHCs): 1 per 30,000 (rural). Staffed by Medical Officer. Currently: 30,000+
- Health & Wellness Centers: Upgraded sub-centers/PHCs providing 12 types of care

SECONDARY LEVEL:
- Community Health Centers (CHCs): 1 per 120,000. 30 beds, 4 specialists. Currently: 6,000+
- Sub-District Hospitals: 31-100 beds
- District Hospitals: 100-500 beds. Currently: 750+

TERTIARY LEVEL:
- Medical College Hospitals: 500+ beds with teaching
- Super-specialty Hospitals: AIIMS (23), INIs
- Regional/National Reference Centers

=== KEY HEALTH INDICATORS AND TARGETS ===

India health targets for 2025-2030:
- Life expectancy: Increase from 69.4 to 72+ years by 2030
- MMR: Below 70 per lakh live births (from current 97)
- IMR: Below 20 per 1000 live births (from current 28)
- Under-5 MR: Below 23 (from current 32)
- Full immunization: 95% (from current 76.4%)
- TB elimination: By 2025 (target likely to extend to 2030)
- Malaria elimination: By 2027 (target likely to extend to 2030)
- OOP expenditure: Below 30% (from current 48%)
- Hospital beds: 2 per 1000 population
- Doctors: 1 per 1000 population
